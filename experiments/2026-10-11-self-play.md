# Self-play, and the named plays

**Status:** COMPLETED

**Proposed:** 2026-10-11

**Activated:** 2026-10-11

**Completed:** 2026-10-12

**Last updated:** 2026-10-12 (closed, with every reading taken; nine recorded duels read again, five against the first long run's last checkpoint and four against With itself's)

Proposed on 2026-10-11, when
[PPO with a head that predicts](2026-10-09-ppo-with-a-head-that-predicts.md)
had every reading taken and the play of its checkpoints, seen in duels on
a web page, had been judged poor (that the checkpoints lost every game, as
this record first had it, is not known: [Why](#why)). It was decided that
day that what comes next is the named plays, with self-play the first
thing to try; the order was agreed, in which the strongest checkpoint is
first measured with what the rate against pilots does not see; who sits at
each seat was decided; and the design and the readings below were
confirmed as they were proposed, before any run. The base was made on the
CUDA machine that day and is read below; the two arms were made after it,
ended on 2026-10-12 and are read below, with the first two readings and
the plays. The exploiters of their last checkpoints were made that day,
and the third reading is read below them. Every reading is taken, and each
came out for self-play: the [conclusion](#conclusion) says what that does
and does not mean.

## Research questions

1. Does a learner that plays half of its duels against itself keep what
   its start wins against the pilots?
2. Does it become a better player of the mirror than the same games
   against the pilots alone make it?
3. Does it leave less for a learner that looks for its habits?
4. Do the named plays change?

## Why

- **Plays of the checkpoints that the pilots do not punish were named.**
  In duels on the web page the first long run's last checkpoint was judged
  to play poorly, and three plays were named: Allure of Darkness activated
  with no DARK monster in hand before the Spells and Traps beside it are
  set, Trap Hole at the first Summon that allows it, and Threatening Roar
  shown before the attack. They are the checkpoints' own
  ([the record](2026-10-09-ppo-with-a-head-that-predicts.md#beside-the-follow-up-duels-in-a-room-2026-10-10)),
  and none costs anything that shows against a pilot, which plays on the
  same whatever it is shown. So what a checkpoint wins against pilots does
  not see them, and more games against pilots give PPO no reason to mend
  them. *Corrected on 2026-10-12:* this said the checkpoint lost every
  game. How many duels were played is not known: that was read from the
  report of them, and a screenshot of the page shows one duel won and one
  lost against a loaded network. The page keeps finished duels only, and
  one record for whatever network is loaded. The judgement of the play
  stands as it was given.
- **The strongest checkpoint has two of the three.** The second long
  run's last checkpoint (81.4% of the held-out deals, where its pilot wins
  60.7%) activates Allure of Darkness with only Spells and Traps beside it
  in 38 of 57 turns, in 35 of them with cards still to set, and takes
  Trap Hole at 74% of the Summons that allow it (its pilot at 89%), at 57%
  for a monster of 1,000 to 1,499 ATK. It plays Threatening Roar at the
  attack, where the first long run's showed it early: that habit was one
  run's path.
- **More PPO against the pilots is running out where the pilots do not
  look.** Against decks outside training the second long run stops
  gaining after 960 updates (0.3 ± 0.7 under), while it gains 3.6 ± 0.9
  on the held-out deals over the same half. In the mirror, with a
  checkpoint in the other seat, the first long run's last checkpoint is
  not shown to be ahead of its checkpoint after 960 updates (51.6% to
  48.4%, ± 1.1), though it wins 3.6 points more against the pilots
  ([head to head](2026-10-09-ppo-with-a-head-that-predicts.md#beside-the-follow-up-head-to-head-2026-10-11)).
- **Self-play learns this game at a scale one card reaches.** ygo-agent's
  trainer, from scratch on the CUDA machine, is at its released model's
  level on one matchup after 356,000 games. It seats the latest network
  at both seats and learns from every step (`scripts/cleanba.py`).
- **The plays are of two kinds, and self-play is expected to mend one**
  (said before any run). Trap Hole at the first chance is a habit an
  opponent can punish, and an opponent that learns has a reason to. Allure
  of Darkness before its cards are set is an error against itself,
  whoever sits opposite, and too rare for a duel's result to carry: it
  wants a look ahead at the decision. So is taking many turns to beat a
  player who does nothing.

## Design (fixed on 2026-10-11)

The start is the second long run's last checkpoint
(`runs/ppo-long-rate/one-pass-long/checkpoints/model-02279.pt` on the CUDA
machine, after 1,920 updates of PPO). Two arms go on from it, each 960
updates of 520 duels, 499,200 games, by PPO in one pass at a learning rate
of 1e-4 on the deals of seed 2012, as the long runs were made:

| Arm | The duels of an update |
| --- | --- |
| **With itself** | 260 against itself: the learner's network at both seats, drawing its answers, the Blackwing deck on both sides, every decision of the duel learned from. 260 against the pilots of the thirteen decks of training, 20 each |
| **Pilots alone** | 520 against the pilots of the thirteen decks, 40 each: the second long run made longer |

- **Who sits at each seat** was decided that day: the latest network at
  both, with half of each update's duels still against the pilots. An
  earlier checkpoint held at one seat is what head to head and the
  exploiters need, and becomes an opponent of training only if a later
  checkpoint is found to lose to an earlier one.
- **Pilots alone is the control.** It has the same games after the start
  and none against itself: what With itself has over it is what self-play
  did, and not what more updates did.
- A checkpoint is kept every 120 updates, and an arm is read by its
  checkpoints after 720, 840 and 960.
- **What the arms differ in beside the seat.** With itself learns from
  about half as many decisions again in an update, both seats of its 260
  duels, and meets each pilot half as often.
- **Limits.** The network plays one deck, so its self-play is the mirror:
  against other decks only the rate against pilots and the plays tell
  anything. One run an arm: the errors are those of the deals, and a run
  has luck of its own that they do not hold (a point or two of the
  held-out deals over 120 updates, by three seeds).

## Measures

- **The held-out deals**: the 1,536 logged deals against the thirteen
  pilots that every experiment since the search's leads is read on
  (`evaluate-search`; the pilot 60.7%, the pilot with its search 72.5%).
- **Head to head**: `eval_suites/blackwing-against-checkpoint.json`, 2,048
  deals of the mirror against a checkpoint held in the other seat, the
  measured seat opening every other deal, each network taking its first
  choice. Two players measured against each other, each in turn the one
  held, play every deal from either seat.
- **An exploiter**: a learner that starts as a checkpoint and plays every
  duel of its updates against that checkpoint held as it is, by PPO as an
  arm's. What its first choices then win of the 2,048 deals against it
  says how far a learner that looks for the checkpoint's habits gets.
- **The named plays**: `tools/named_plays.py`, 2,240 games against pilots
  and 120 against a player who does nothing, with Allure of Darkness told
  by the turn, Trap Hole by the window and Threatening Roar by where it is
  activated.
- **The decks outside training**:
  `eval_suites/ygo-policies-blackwing-unseen.json`, 2,304 games against
  the pilots of eighteen decks.
- As an arm goes: every 120 updates the suite against the thirteen
  pilots, and the mirror against the start held in the other seat.

## The base

Before any arm the start is measured with what the arms are then measured
with. No reading hangs on it.

- **Head to head**, measured on the development machine with the
  checkpoints at hand
  ([the record before](2026-10-09-ppo-with-a-head-that-predicts.md#beside-the-follow-up-head-to-head-2026-10-11)):
  the start wins 54.3% ± 0.7 of 4,096 games against the first long run's
  last checkpoint, every deal from either seat; its pilot wins 21.4% ± 0.9
  against it and the copy PPO started from 18.2% ± 0.9. Added on the CUDA
  machine: the earlier checkpoints of its own run and the start itself
  against it.
- **The plays** of the start are counted
  ([the record before](2026-10-09-ppo-with-a-head-that-predicts.md#beside-the-follow-up-duels-in-a-room-2026-10-10),
  the last column of its table).
- **Exploiters of the start**: two, their duels drawn with the training
  seeds 2012 and 2013, each 240 updates of 520 duels on from the start
  itself, measured every 30 updates against the start and against the
  pilots. They tell how far an exploiter gets, how far two of them end
  apart, and what one loses against the pilots as it goes.

Made on the CUDA machine in three hours and a quarter
(`analysis/self-play/`), at the pinned revision of the pilots.

**Head to head.** Against the start held in the other seat, the 2,048
deals of the mirror, first choices on both sides. A checkpoint of the
start's own run is told by the updates of PPO it has had (its file counts
the 360 of imitation before them too). The last column is what a player
wins of those deals less what the start itself wins of them, deal by deal.

| after 960 | 78.0% | 48.3% ± 1.1 | +0.1 ± 1.3 |

- **The start against itself wins 48.2% of these deals, not half.** With
  one network at both seats the seat that opens wins 59.6% of them, and
  the measured seat opens every other one: what is left is which deals
  fall to it. Half is exact only when every deal is played from either
  seat, as the second reading has it.
- **More PPO against the pilots did not make a better player of the
  mirror.** The start's last 1,440 updates added 6.5 points of the
  held-out deals. With the start in the other seat its checkpoint after
  480 updates wins what the start itself wins there (1.3 ± 1.3 under),
  and the one after 1,440 wins more (4.8 ± 1.3 over; one of five
  comparisons, each deal from one seat). The first long run showed the
  same of its second half.

**The exploiters.** At each evaluation: what an exploiter's first choices
win of the 2,048 deals against the start (± 1.0 to 1.1; the start itself
48.2%), what they win of the suite's 1,664 games against the thirteen
pilots (± 1.0 to 1.1; the start 79.4%), and what the exploiter won of the
duels it trained on since the evaluation before, drawing its answers.

- **An exploiter gets far, and most of the way at once.** 12 points over
  what the start itself wins after 30 updates (15,600 games), 17 after
  60, and 21 to 22 after 240, where it is still rising: 69.3% and 68.1%
  over the last three evaluations. From either seat: after 240 updates
  the two win 75.8% and 75.7% of the deals they open (the start itself
  57.8%) and 64.3% and 63.0% of those they play second (38.6%).
- **Two exploiters of one checkpoint go alike.** They end 1.2 points
  apart over their last three evaluations, and are 0.1 to 3.0 apart at
  the eight (1.0 on average), about what the error of the deals allows
  by itself.
- **What they find is the start's own.** Against the Blackwing pilot, the
  same matchup, they win what the start wins: 103 of the suite's 128
  games for the start, 96 to 107 at all sixteen evaluations (100 and 104
  on average). It is not a better game of the mirror that they learn, or
  little of one: on 2,048 deals the first exploiter wins 2.5 ± 1.1 more
  than the start against that pilot
  ([below](#what-an-exploiter-of-the-start-uses-2026-10-11)).
- **They pay for it against the decks they no longer meet**: of the 1,536
  games against the other twelve pilots the start wins 79.3%, the
  exploiters 71.3% and 69.4% after 240 updates.
- **Which of the start's habits they use** is not in a run's figures,
  which count kinds of answers: theirs are the start's with more attacks
  (6.5 and 6.3 a game for 5.1) and fewer monsters set (0.3 and 0.4 for
  0.7). It was read from the first exploiter's last checkpoint
  ([below](#what-an-exploiter-of-the-start-uses-2026-10-11)).
- **Answers drawn lose to first choices.** In its first update an
  exploiter is the start drawing its answers against the start's first
  choices, and wins 44.3% ± 1.5 of the 1,040 duels of the two. So the
  duels a run trains on are under what its first choices win.
- **Time.** An exploiter's 240 updates take 1.6 hours with their eight
  evaluations of 75 s: 21 to 22 s an update, 84,000 to 90,000 duels an
  hour, where an update with a pilot in the other seat takes 14.0 s
  (134,000). The games are what is longer, 12 to 13 s for 6.4 s: the
  engines take the same (3.3 to 3.5 s for 2.9 s) and the two networks'
  answers 7 to 8 s where the one's take 2.5 s, in twice as many batches
  of half the size. The update itself takes 5.9 s for 5.1 s, of 53,000
  decisions for 45,000. The mirror's 2,048 deals with a network at both
  seats take 41 s. (Corrected on 2026-10-11: this said 12 to 13 s an
  update, which is the games alone.)

**What it says for the arms.** The start is far from a player a learner
finds nothing against: 125,000 games against it alone, a quarter of what
an arm plays, are worth over 20 points. That is the room the third
research question asks about. Two exploiters of one checkpoint end about a
point apart, which is what the size of the third reading is set by.

### What an exploiter of the start uses (2026-10-11)

The last checkpoint of the first exploiter (training seed 2012, after 240
updates) was copied to the development machine. Its games against the
start were played again there, one decision at a time, on the 2,048 deals
of the mirror, with every decision of either seat kept and, at each of the
exploiter's, the answer the start would give in its place. No reading
hangs on it. The replays, the scripts that made and read them and their
tables are in `analysis/exploiter-read/` on that machine, not in the
repository.

- **The replay is the card's games, but for near-ties.** One decision at a
  time the exploiter wins 1,436 of the deals (1,434 on the card) and the
  start against itself 982 (987): 15 of the start's deals end otherwise,
  where two answers lie so close that a batch rounds them the other way.
  Against the Blackwing pilot the suite's 128 games come out as the card
  has them (103 for the start, 102 for the exploiter). With every deal
  from either seat the exploiter wins 69.8% ± 0.7 of 4,096 games.
- **Its answer is not the start's at 12.1% of its decisions**: at 34.8% of
  the commands of a Main Phase, 4.9% of the chain windows and 4.0% of the
  commands of a Battle Phase.

**Mixed players.** A mixed player takes the start's answers but where a
rule says the exploiter's; the selections a command leads to go with the
command. Each plays the start on the same 2,048 deals, and the last column
is deal by deal.

| Player | Wins | Over the start |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| The start | 47.9% | |
| The start with the exploiter's commands of a Battle Phase | 49.0% | +1.1 ± 0.6 |
| with its answers at chain windows | 52.5% | +4.5 ± 1.0 |
| with its commands of a Main Phase | 64.4% | +16.4 ± 1.3 |
| The start, making Blackwing Armor Master where the exploiter would | 50.7% | +2.7 ± 0.7 |
| and playing as the exploiter while it has one on the field | 54.6% | +6.7 ± 0.8 |
| The exploiter, never making Armor Master | 62.2% | +14.2 ± 1.3 |
| The exploiter | 70.1% | +22.2 ± 1.3 |

The commands of a Main Phase by what the start's would be, the exploiter's
taken in its place, each class alone:

| Where the start would | Wins | Over the start |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| end the turn | 52.7% | +4.8 ± 0.9 |
| Normal Summon | 52.1% | +4.1 ± 1.0 |
| enter the Battle Phase | 51.7% | +3.7 ± 1.0 |
| set a monster | 51.4% | +3.4 ± 0.8 |
| activate a card or an effect | 50.1% | +2.1 ± 0.9 |
| set a Spell or Trap | 49.4% | +1.5 ± 0.7 |
| Special Summon | 49.1% | +1.2 ± 0.7 |

- **Three quarters of the gain is in the commands of a Main Phase** and a
  fifth at the chain windows; the three kinds add up to the whole (22.0
  of 22.2).
- **Blackwing Armor Master is the largest single thing, a third of it.**
  It is a Synchro Monster that is not destroyed by battle and whose
  controller takes no damage from its battles. The exploiter makes it in
  891 of its 2,048 games, 91% of those that offer it, first at turn 5.7
  on average, and attacks with it 1.8 times a game. The start makes it in
  a fifth of the games that offer it, at 5% to 6% of the decisions that
  do, first at turn 8 to 9. Without it the exploiter wins 8.0 ± 0.8
  fewer. The start with it wins 6.7 ± 0.8 more played as the exploiter
  plays it and 2.7 ± 0.7 more with the Summon alone: left to itself the
  start does not attack with it as the exploiter does.
- **The start does not answer it.** An Armor Master of the exploiter
  stays 7.3 turns on the field on average (the median is 4) and is there
  at the last decision of 327 games, of which the exploiter wins 299. In
  1,906 of the 6,175 turns in which the start decides with one face-up
  before it, the turn offers it Icarus Attack, Dimensional Prison or Dark
  Hole, and it takes one in 591 of them (31%): Dark Hole in 161 of the 764
  turns that offer it (21%), Icarus Attack in 276 of 1,038 (27%),
  Dimensional Prison in 155 of 195 (79%).
- **Against the Blackwing pilot the card is worth little.** The start
  with Armor Master, played as the exploiter plays it, wins 1.0 ± 0.5
  more of the same deals against the pilot than the start does. The
  weakness is the start's answer to the card more than the card it
  leaves unmade.
- **The rest is spread.** No class of the start's commands carries it:
  each alone is worth 1 to 5 points, and together they overlap (20.8
  beside the 16.4 of all of them). The plainest: the start sets its Jin
  the Rain Shadow face-down 0.65 times a game and the exploiter 0.29;
  where the start would Normal Summon the exploiter enters the Battle
  Phase without at 1,527 decisions; where the start would enter it the
  exploiter first sets a Spell or Trap at 1,404. At the chain windows
  it takes Bottomless Trap Hole at 96% of those that offer it (the start
  64%), sets Mystical Space Typhoon and chains it (0.47 times a game for
  0.24) where the start plays it from the hand, and holds its Kalut the
  Moon Shadow on the other player's turn (19% of its windows for 36%).
- **The named plays are not what it uses.** The start takes Trap Hole at
  77% of the windows that offer it against the exploiter as against itself
  (935 of 1,218; 944 of 1,219). More of them are on a monster of 1,000 to
  1,499 ATK (306 for 216, 241 taken for 176), which is 65 Trap Holes in
  2,048 games. The exploiter has the plays itself: it activates Allure of
  Darkness with only Spells and Traps beside it in 18 of 27 turns, in 17
  of them with cards still to set.
- **Most of it is the start's own, now on more deals.** Against the
  Blackwing pilot on the same 2,048 deals the exploiter wins 78.9% and
  the start 76.4%: 2.5 ± 1.1 more, where it wins 22.2 more against the
  start.

**What it says.** A learner that looks for the start's habits does not
find the named plays. It finds a card the start neither makes nor answers,
and many smaller things in what the start commits in a Main Phase. What an
exploiter finds and the named plays so measure different things, as the
third reading and the fourth have them. With itself is the arm that meets
its own Armor Master: whether it then answers it is in what its exploiters
still find, and can be counted in its last checkpoint's games as it is
here.

**Limits.** One exploiter of the two, at its last checkpoint. A mixed
player's gain is not a part of a sum where the classes overlap. A replay
one decision at a time departs from the card's games in about a hundredth
of them.

## Readings (fixed on 2026-10-11)

Proposed and confirmed before any run, but for the size of the third,
which was proposed with the base read and confirmed that day, before any
figure of an arm was seen. The first two are taken by With itself's
checkpoints after 720, 840 and 960 updates.

1. *Self-play keeps what it has against the pilots.* Those three
   checkpoints against the start on the 1,536 held-out deals, deal by
   deal: for each deal the share of the three that win it, less whether
   the start wins it. It has **lost** when it wins at least 2 points and
   two standard errors fewer; it has kept it otherwise.
2. *Self-play makes the better player of the mirror.* Those three
   checkpoints against Pilots alone's checkpoint after 960 updates, on the
   2,048 deals of the mirror, each in turn the one held in the other
   seat: 12,288 games, every deal played from either seat by each of the
   three. For each deal the share of its six games that With itself wins,
   a drawn game being half a win, and the standard error over the deals.
   It is **better** when it wins at least 52% of the games and two
   standard errors over half, **worse** when at most 48% and two under,
   and between the two no difference is shown.
3. *Self-play leaves less for an exploiter.* What exploiters win against
   With itself's checkpoint after 960 updates and against Pilots alone's.
   Its size was left to be fixed when the base is read, since the base
   tells how far two exploiters of one checkpoint end apart, and before
   any exploiter of an arm is made. **Fixed on 2026-10-11: proposed with
   the base read and confirmed that day, with the arms still running and
   no figure of them seen.**
   - *The exploiters.* Two of each of the two checkpoints, training seeds
     2012 and 2013, made as the base's of the start: each starts as its
     victim and plays 240 updates of 520 duels, every duel against the
     victim held, with a checkpoint every 30.
   - *What is compared.* An exploiter's checkpoints after 180, 210 and 240
     updates against its victim on the 2,048 deals of the mirror, each in
     turn the one held: 12,288 games, every deal from either seat by each
     of the three, a drawn game half a win. For a deal, an arm's figure is
     the share of its twelve games that its two exploiters win.
   - *The number that decides.* With itself's figure less Pilots alone's,
     deal by deal, with its standard error over the deals. Self-play
     leaves **less** when its exploiters win at least 3 points fewer and
     two standard errors, and each of its two wins fewer than each of the
     other two; **more** when the same holds the other way; between the
     two no difference is shown.
   - *Why these.* Two exploiters of the start end 1.2 points apart and an
     exploiter gains about 20 over an even game: 3 points are beyond one
     exploiter's luck and a seventh of what there is to find. Every deal
     from either seat makes an even game exactly half, which a deal from
     one seat does not (the start against itself wins 48.2%).
   - *What it does not see.* The victim plays by its first choice, as a
     checkpoint does wherever it is measured or seated in a room: what a
     player gains by varying its answers is not in this measure.
4. Without a mark: the named plays, for the start and the last checkpoint
   of each arm. Self-play is expected to move Trap Hole and not Allure of
   Darkness ([Why](#why)).

Told beside them, without a mark: Pilots alone against the start, and With
itself against Pilots alone, on the held-out deals; each arm against the
start in the mirror, as it goes; and the decks outside training. With the
exploiters: what each wins against the pilots as it goes, and whether what
the start's exploiters found is still there, the start and the last
checkpoint of each of its two exploiters against each arm's last
checkpoint, every deal from either seat.

## The run

From the repository root on the CUDA machine, with nothing else running
there:

```sh
bash experiments/commands/self-play.sh
PARTS=arms bash experiments/commands/self-play.sh
PARTS=exploiters bash experiments/commands/self-play.sh
```

The first is the base, the second the arms and their measurements, the
third the exploiters of the arms' last checkpoints, for the third reading
as it was fixed. A measurement or a run
that is there is not made again, and an interrupted run goes on. The base
took three hours and a quarter. The arms took twelve hours less ten
minutes, where about eleven were reckoned from what the same games take
with a pilot in the other seat: With itself 6.9, Pilots alone 4.1, the
measurements 0.9. The exploiters took eight and a half, where about seven
were reckoned (1.6 hours each by the base, four of them, and 48
measurements of under a minute): an exploiter of an arm took 1.7 to 2.2.
`analysis/self-play/` is what is copied back.

The pin of `native/ygo-policies` stays at `1b85b63` while this is open:
the held-out deals are logged games of that revision.

### What the start's two plays are worth in games (2026-10-12)

How much do the plays cost the start in games won? Measured on the
development machine by playing the start with a play forbidden. The start
plays a deal by its first choice. Where a first choice is the play, the
same deal is played a second time with that answer forbidden wherever the
rule names it: the network is shown the decision as always, and its best
answer among the others is taken. A game without the play in it is the
same game either way. In the other seat is the start as it is, every deal
from either seat, or a pilot, the thirteen of training and Machina with
the seats in turn. This is outside the trainer: nothing a network is
offered in training is filtered.

The rules, from what `tools/named_plays.py` counts:

- *Allure of Darkness*: not activated, from the hand or from where it was
  set, while the hand has no DARK monster and the decision still offers
  another Spell or Trap to set.
- *Trap Hole, a weak monster*: not activated at a Summon whose monster has
  under 1,500 ATK.
- *Trap Hole, the first chance*: not activated at the first window of a
  game that offers it.

  Summons Jin the Rain Shadow in Attack Position instead (600 ATK, lost to
  an attack the next turn for 1,000), then a Shura the Blue Flame, then
  spends Dark Hole on one monster, after which the card needs a Tribute
  again. The same happened in a duel the other checkpoint lost. Over the
  nine duels a network could Summon the card so twelve times and did
  seven.

- **Keeping Sirocco the Dawn is not what loses.** It had been read as the
  mistake of the duel it lost. Over thousands of games the checkpoint wins
  no more with the card Summoned wherever it needs no Tribute, and a
  little less: what it does in its place (a Shura the Blue Flame most

- **It leaves an attack its pilot would make in one game in ten**, where
  the first long run's last checkpoint does in one in seven and the start
  in one in fifteen, and wins no more with those attacks made.
- **Threatening Roar spent early** is in 8 of its 4,096 games against

**What it says.** Everything seen or named in these checkpoints' play, and
what was taken from reading single duels, measures as nothing or as better
than the play set against it: Allure of Darkness before its cards are set,
Trap Hole at the first chance, Threatening Roar held or spent early,
attacks not made, Sirocco the Dawn kept. Reading a lost duel for its
mistake has now been wrong twice here. What beats these checkpoints in
duels is not found yet.

**Limits.** Four duels. The play set against the checkpoint's is its
pilot's or a rule's, which is not the best play, and the opponents
measured against do not change with it. The review and what it kept are in
`analysis/person-duels/out-with-itself/` on the development machine, the
measurements in `analysis/habit-mask/`.

## Conclusion

**What it answered.** A learner given half of each update's duels against
itself, the latest network at both seats, for 960 updates on from the
strongest checkpoint:

1. *kept what its start wins against the pilots*, by the mark fixed: 1.4
   ± 0.9 points fewer of the held-out deals. Beside the same updates
   against the pilots alone it is about two points under, on the decks of
   training (2.1 ± 0.7) and outside them (2.3 ± 0.5);
2. *became the better player of the mirror, by far*: 67.5% ± 0.5 of its
   games against that control, and 70% against its own start;
3. *left next to nothing for a learner that looks for its habits*: such a
   learner ends 1.1 ± 0.4 over an even game with it, where it ends 17
   over the control and 19 over the start, and what such a learner had
   found in the start no longer beats it;
4. *and plays the named plays as before.* They are the same in both arms,
   and the start wins no more against itself or the pilots without them.

The three readings came out for self-play, the second and third by
margins far outside what one run an arm leaves open. The two points
against the pilots are what one pair of runs does not settle.

**What it does not say.**

- *How it plays in duels on the page.* The experiment began from a
  judgement of the play in such duels, and when this was written With
  itself's checkpoint had not been played in any. It has been since, four
  duels ([above](#four-recorded-duels-against-with-itself-2026-10-12)),
  which is no measure of whether it is harder to beat. The named plays are
  still its plays.
- *What an exploiter of another kind finds.* These are our own learner,
  240 updates on from the victim, against a victim that plays its first
  choice. More updates, a network that starts elsewhere, or a search
  might find what they did not.
- *Other matchups.* Everything measured against a network is the
  Blackwing mirror.
- *Whether two points against the pilots are the price.* One run an arm,
  of one share of the duels: another share, or more updates, was not
  tried.

**Closed on 2026-10-12**, with every reading taken. What comes next, as
chosen that day: first whether one network holds several decks
([One network, several decks](2026-10-12-one-network-several-decks.md)), a
run of two hours whose marks were confirmed on 2026-10-10; then, as an
experiment of its own and a run for a night, the two arms again with a
second seed and With itself for more updates, which is what settles the
two points against the pilots and says whether the gain goes on.

What the experiment leaves to build on:

- With itself's last checkpoint
  (`runs/self-play/with-itself/checkpoints/model-03239.pt` on the CUDA

## The tooling

- **The trainer** seats a network in the other seat since 2026-10-11
  ([docs/commands.md](../docs/commands.md), its section on who plays the
  other seat). `--self-play-duels N` shares `N` of an update's duels out
  to the learner against itself: each seat has its own result and one wins
  what the other loses. `--opponent-checkpoint` holds a checkpoint as it
  is, which plays by its first choice in `--opponent-checkpoint-duels` of
  an update's duels, of which only the learner's decisions are learned
  from, and in the cases of a suite that say so (`opponent_policy`:
  `checkpoint`), for `train` and `evaluate-suite`. A run without these
  options plays the games it played: the tests that hold the pool's games
  to the workers' pass as they did. Held to one-duel-at-a-time play: a
  game against itself or against a held checkpoint is the duel played one
  decision at a time, and a duel against a held checkpoint is the same
  duel told for the other seat.
- `experiments/commands/self-play.sh` makes the base, the arms and the
  exploiters of the arms, and `tools/self_play_report.py` tells them and
  takes the readings, the first two only of an arm that has had its 960
  updates and the third only of two whole exploiters of each arm's last
  checkpoint. Tried on the development machine at a small size, to the
  end and again.
- `tools/named_plays.py` counts the plays. On the decisions kept of the
  games counted by hand it gives every figure recorded, and it plays the
  same games.
