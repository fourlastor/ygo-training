# Overlap: the pass beside the next update's games

**Status:** COMPLETED (closed on 2026-10-23, the day it was read, as the
Cyber champion was to begin when it was finished)

**Proposed:** 2026-10-23

**Activated:** 2026-10-23, when [Duels in flight](2026-10-23-duels-in-flight.md)
was closed. **The design and the readings were fixed before anything was
built.**

**Last updated:** 2026-10-23 (run and read: equivalent and not faster;
closed, not made a default)

The run `fast` of duels in flight plays an update in 6.79 s: its games 2.73
s (the model's calls in them 1.21), the pass 3.01 and the rest 1.05. The
games wait on the pass, and the card on the games. PufferLib, and Cleanba
before it, play the next update's games beside the pass, with weights one
update behind. It was planned on 2026-10-23 for after duels in flight, if
those were on par with the baseline, and stayed the next step once two
groups were kept.

## Research questions

1. Does PPO with the next update's games played beside the pass learn as
   well per duel as `fast` did?
2. How much faster is an update?

## Design (fixed on 2026-10-23)

- **What changes.** While update u's pass runs, a second thread plays update
  u+1's games with a copy of the weights from before that pass. At update
  u+1 the trainer waits for those games, gives the second thread the weights
  the pass left, and lets it begin u+2's. A duel's answers are one update
  older than in `fast`: begun about two updates before it is trained on,
  where `fast`'s were begun one before.
- **What does not.** PPO's ratio is taken against the chances its game was
  answered with, as it already is. Nothing else of the update changes. No
  V-trace: that would be a second change, and it is the follow-up if this
  reads worse.
- **The build.** An option of the trainer, with a mode that does the same in
  turn on one thread, and a test that holds the two together update for
  update, to the bit, on the processor. A lock goes around the pool's calls,
  which the games and the pass's minibatches both make. On a card the second
  thread's calls go on a CUDA stream of their own.
- **One arm, `overlap`.** `fast`'s settings (the default on a card since
  2026-10-23) and the option, from `all71`'s copy to R1 (1,920 updates of
  PPO), as `fast` went. It is measured against `fast` there. `fast`'s
  measurements stand, so only `overlap`'s checkpoints are measured; its
  last checkpoint is measured again on one screen at the arm's code, and
  must give the same games or both are measured anew.
- **A trial first,** at the same code: 20 updates of `fast`'s settings and 20
  of `overlap`'s.
- **What is expected.** An update takes at best the larger of the games and
  the pass with the rest, about 4.1 s, 1.67 times as fast. By reckoning it
  will be 1.25 to 1.4 times, as the second thread's calls share the card
  with the pass, and Python's threads share one lock.
- **Where.** On the CUDA machine, after the engine there is built from a
  profile of its own games (`tools/engine_pgo_build.sh`, decided on
  2026-10-23), so the trial's two parts and the arm run the same build.

## Readings (fixed on 2026-10-23, before anything was built)

1. *It learns as well*: `overlap` against `fast` at R1, both by their
   checkpoints after 1,680, 1,800 and 1,920 updates of PPO, game by game
   over the three suites together (33,696 games): **equivalent** when the
   gap less two standard errors is above −2 points, **worse** when the gap
   plus two standard errors is below −2 points, otherwise **not decided**.
2. *It is faster*: `overlap`'s first 20 updates against the trial's 20 of
   `fast`'s settings, at the same code and from the same start, by duels an
   hour: **faster** when it plays at least 1.25 times as many.

Told beside them, without a mark:

- the whole run's duels an hour, and how an update's time parts between
  the games, the model's calls, the pass and the rest;
- how long the pass and the games went together, and what each waited for;
- how old a duel is when it is trained on;
- how often PPO's clip binds, against `fast`;
- each suite and screen;
- the duels cut off.

## Plan

Built on the development machine and tried small on its processor, with the
test against the one-thread mode. Then on the CUDA machine, in one command
(`experiments/commands/overlap.sh`): the trial, the arm, the measurement
and the report.

## Results (2026-10-23)

The trial's 40 updates ran from 16:48, the arm's 1,920 updates of PPO from
16:52 to 19:51, and the command measured them by 20:36, at `65f6ebad` (the
report and the runs' metrics copied to `analysis/overlap/run/`). `fast`'s
last checkpoint, played again on the Blackwing screen at this code, won the
same games, so `fast`'s measurements stood.

1. **It learns as well: equivalent.** `overlap` against `fast` at R1, by
   their checkpoints after 1,680, 1,800 and 1,920 updates of PPO, game by
   game over the three suites: 55.4% against 55.2%, +0.2 ± 0.2 over 33,696
   games. With two standard errors taken away the gap is −0.2, above −2.
2. **It is faster: not faster.** Its first 20 updates played 328,605 duels
   an hour, the trial's 20 of `fast`'s settings at the same code 280,118:
   1.17 times, under 1.25.

Beside them, without a mark:

- **The suites:** the thirteen screens +0.3 ± 0.2 (55.7% against 55.4%,
  21,632 games), the eighteen other decks of thirty-one +0.4 ± 0.5 (50.4%
  against 50.0%, 3,744 games), the forty new decks −0.1 ± 0.4 (56.8%
  against 56.9%, 8,320 games). Screen by screen from −1.5 (Gishki) to +2.0
  (Monarch).
- **The whole run:** 329,808 duels an hour against `fast`'s 275,662 (1.20
  times), 5.68 s an update against 6.79. The games beside the pass took
  3.89 s (the model's calls in them 2.16), where `fast`'s took 2.73 (1.21)
  before it, and the pass 4.57 s against 3.01.
- **What each side waited for:** the update never waited for its games
  (0.00 s an update); the games waited 0.10 s an update for the pass's
  calls into the pool, the pass 0.00 s for theirs; the weights were copied
  in under a millisecond.
- **How old a duel is when it is trained on:** begun 1.00 update before on
  average, 25 at most (`fast` 1.00 and 30), its answers given by weights one
  update older than `fast`'s.
- **PPO's clip** bound at 0.021 of its decisions, against 0.017.
- **Against the pilots in training:** level with `fast` along the run, from
  50.9% against 51.0% over the first 240 updates to 57.4% against 57.8%
  over the last 240.
- **Cut off:** 13 duels (`fast` 9).
- **The card's memory:** at most 6.0 GiB of its 12 in use, but 86 warnings
  of PyTorch's that an allocation found the card full and was made once its
  cache was let go (`fast`'s run had one); none failed.

What it says: the next update's games played beside the pass learn per duel
as `fast` did, by the rule, but take only a sixth off an update. The update
is now the pass's, and the pass is half as long again (4.6 s against 3.0)
with the games sharing the card and Python's lock with it.

## Closed (2026-10-23)

Before the readings were in, it was settled that the champion run would
begin when this one was finished, with overlap in its configuration if both
readings passed and otherwise with `fast`'s settings alone. It met the first
reading and not the second, so it is not made a default and the champion
runs without it; the option stays, off unless asked for. The next
experiment is
