# A network of two decks at the champions' games of each mirror

**Status:** ACTIVE

**Proposed:** 2026-10-24

**Activated:** 2026-10-24, when [A Cyber champion](2026-10-23-cyber-champion.md)
was read and closed. The question: whether a network of two decks, given as
many duels of each pairing as a network of one deck has, matches both
single-deck champions head to head, at about half of the games against
each; which tells, in part, whether duels per pairing measure what a
comparable strength needs. A design with its duels against itself in the
mirrors only was set aside for one that keeps the two decks against each
other, at four times a champion's updates of PPO: the setup a network of
every deck would need, at a smaller scale. The mirrors only were left for
later. **The design and the readings were fixed before anything was
built.**

**Last updated:** 2026-10-24 (the mirrors only read: they match both
champions and are ahead of both against `all71`, on the champions' games
alone)

The recipe of the network of Blackwing alone (`decks-01` of
[More decks](2026-10-14-more-decks.md)) made a champion of Cyber too:
its pilot copied for 240 updates, then 720 updates of PPO with half of each
update's 520 duels the mirror against itself, 74.4% and 76.4% of the Cyber
mirror against `all71` in its two arms. More decks gave Blackwing to
networks of 2 to 13 decks at equal updates, so with fewer games of its
mirror; their record could not tell a game against itself on another deck
that counts for less from a network that plays the mirror worse for sharing
its weights. Here each deck has as many games of its mirror as its
champion had.

## Research question

Does one network of Blackwing and Cyber, made by the champion's recipe with
as many games of each mirror as each deck's champion had, and the two decks
against each other besides, play each mirror as well as that deck's
champion?

## Design (fixed on 2026-10-24)

- **The network of both (`both`).** One network of Blackwing and Cyber, the
  pilots as pinned. Its copy as the champion's, 480 updates (240 a deck), so
  each pairing with a pilot has its 9,600 duels. Then PPO at 1e-4 for 2,880
  updates, 720 for each ordered pair of its decks. Of an update's 520
  duels, 260 are against itself, on every ordered pair of its decks as
  often (65 an update each: the two mirrors, Blackwing against Cyber and
  Cyber against Blackwing). The other 260 are against the thirteen decks'
  pilots, 10 an update in each of its 26 pairings. So each mirror has
  187,200 games, as a champion's 720 updates; the two decks meet 374,400
  times; and each pairing with a pilot has 28,800 duels, twice a
  champion's. `fast`'s settings, the default on a card. A checkpoint every
  480 updates.
- **Blackwing alone (`blackwing`), afresh.** The champion's recipe for
  Blackwing at today's code: the copy of 240 updates, then 720 of PPO, with
  the same settings and a checkpoint every 120. `decks-01` was made at the
  code of 2026-10-14 (format 14, other pins, before the rules of commands
  given again and of negated monsters); it is measured against the new
  one beside the readings.
- **The Cyber champion** is `cyber-old`, which copied the pilot as pinned,
  as `both` does.
- Seed 2012 for all, on the CUDA machine, one after the other: its 20
  threads would be shared by two at once.
- **Measured afterwards** (`experiments/commands/two-decks.sh`), each
  pairing on 2,048 deals, every deal from both seats: `both` against each
  deck's champion in its mirror at as many games of it, by the last three
  checkpoints; `both` and Blackwing alone against `all71` in the mirrors;
  `decks-01`, folded, against Blackwing alone; `both` playing Blackwing
  against `all71` playing Cyber, and the other way round
  (`eval_suites/blackwing-against-cyber-checkpoint.json`).

## Readings (fixed on 2026-10-24, before anything was built)

1. *Blackwing*: `both` against Blackwing alone in the Blackwing mirror, by
   their checkpoints at as many games of it (`both` after 1,920, 2,400 and
   2,880 updates of PPO, Blackwing alone after 480, 600 and 720), every
   deal from both seats, a deal's games together: **matches** when its
   share less two standard errors is at least 48%, **weaker** when its
   share plus two standard errors is at most 48%, otherwise **not
   decided**.
2. *Cyber*: the same in the Cyber mirror, against `cyber-old`.

Told beside them, without a mark (`tools/two_decks_report.py`): each
mirror against `all71`, `both` and the deck's champion paired deal by deal
(`cyber-old`'s from its own record); Blackwing against Cyber, `both` playing
each deck against `all71`; two runs of the recipe head to head, `cyber-new`
against `cyber-old` (below) and `decks-01` against Blackwing alone; the
hours.

## The calibration (2026-10-24)

While the readings were being fixed, two runs of the recipe were played
head to head, to see how far apart they land. `cyber-new` against `cyber-old` in the Cyber mirror, checkpoint for
checkpoint after 480, 600 and 720 updates of PPO, every deal from both
seats (`analysis/cyber-champion/calibration`): `cyber-new` wins **57.3% ±
0.5%** (55.7%, 55.5% and 60.7%), though `cyber-old` wins 2.0 ± 0.5 points
more of the mirror against `all71`. Their games are long, 18 to 24 turns,
4% to 10% of them decided by a deck-out.

The two copied different pilots, so this is not the spread of seeds alone,
but it is wider than the readings' 2 points: by their rule `cyber-old`
would be weaker than `cyber-new`. A reading of weaker by less than some 7
points is to be read with that in mind; one of matches says what it says.
A second run of Blackwing alone on another seed, for a spread of seeds
alone (about 1.5 hours more), was put off. `cyber-old` stays the Cyber
champion, as it copied the same pilot as `both`, and the night ran as
designed, the readings as fixed. The 57.3% may also be a difference of
strength; telling the two apart is left for later.

## Results (2026-10-24)

The network of both ran from 00:47 to 07:21 and Blackwing alone to 09:12;
both were measured by 09:38, at
`08262225`'s code (the report and the runs' metrics copied to
`analysis/two-decks/`). Nothing failed: no duel stopped by the engine or cut
off, no memory warnings, at most 0.6 GiB of the card in use.

1. **Blackwing: matches.** The network of both wins 52.3% ± 0.4% of the
   Blackwing mirror against Blackwing alone, checkpoint for checkpoint at
   as many games of the mirror (52.3%, 52.3% and 52.1%). With two standard
   errors taken away that is 51.5%, over 48%.
2. **Cyber: matches.** It wins 57.2% ± 0.5% of the Cyber mirror against
   `cyber-old` (58.6%, 55.1% and 57.8%); less two standard errors, 56.2%.

Beside them, without a mark:

- **Against `all71`, the deals paired with each champion's own:** in the
  Blackwing mirror the network of both wins 67.8%, Blackwing alone 64.8%,
  +3.0 ± 0.6 points; in the Cyber mirror 81.3% against `cyber-old`'s 76.4%,
  +4.9 ± 0.5. Both rise along its last three checkpoints (67.0% to 68.4%,
  80.5% to 82.0%).
- **Blackwing against Cyber:** the network of both, playing either deck
  against `all71` playing the other, 63.5% ± 0.5% (62.1%, 63.7% and 64.7%).
- **Two runs of the recipe head to head:** `decks-01`, made at the code of
  2026-10-14 and played folded, against Blackwing alone 53.6% ± 0.4%;
  `cyber-new` against `cyber-old` 57.3% ± 0.5%.
- **The hours:** the network of both 0.48 of copy and 6.09 of PPO, Blackwing
  alone 0.27 and 1.58. The network of both's updates grew from 5.7 s to 8.7
  s as its games against itself went from 12 to 16 turns, Blackwing
  alone's from 6.3 s to 10.2 s (14 to 18 turns); neither had a phase of
  long games like `cyber-new`'s.
- **Head to head the games run long:** 15 to 20 turns, up to 8% of them
  decided by a deck-out; against `all71`, 11 to 14 turns.

What it says: given as many games of each mirror as each champion had, and
the two decks against each other besides, one network of two decks plays
each mirror at least as well as that deck's own champion. Against `all71`,
an opponent common to both, it does better than either champion, by more
than the 2 points that parted the two Cyber runs there. Head to head alone
its margins are within what two runs of the recipe show, so by the
readings it is at least as strong, and by the common opponent stronger. It
took twice the updates of the two champions together (2,880 against 720
each), as half of its duels against itself were the two decks against each
other and its pilots' pairings had twice a champion's duels. What part of
its edge came from those and what part from the shared weights, the run
does not tell; a network of both with its duels against itself in the
mirrors only would, the run left for later.

## Follow-up: the mirrors only (design and readings fixed on 2026-10-24)

The run left for later, taken up once the readings were in. Recorded here
as this experiment's follow-up, which stays the active one.

- **The network of the mirrors (`mirrors`).** One network of Blackwing and
  Cyber with as many games of each mirror, and of each pairing with a
  pilot, as the two champions together, and no more. PPO from the copy of
  the network of both (`both-copy`, the same copy) for 1,440 updates at
  1e-4. Of an update's 520 duels, 260 are against itself, 130 in each
  deck's mirror (`--self-play-pairs mirrors`, built for it), and 260
  against the thirteen decks' pilots, 10 in each of its 26 pairings. So it
  has 187,200 games of each mirror and 14,400 duels of each pairing with a
  pilot, as each champion, and no game of Blackwing against Cyber.
  `fast`'s settings; a checkpoint every 240 updates. About 3 to 3.5 hours.
- **Measured** (`experiments/commands/two-decks-mirrors.sh`), `mirrors`
  after 960, 1,200 and 1,440 updates of PPO, as many games of each mirror
  as a champion after 480, 600 and 720 and the network of both after
  1,920, 2,400 and 2,880; every deal from both seats: against `all71` in
  each mirror and in Blackwing against Cyber, and against each champion
  and the network of both in each mirror.

### Readings (fixed on 2026-10-24, before anything was built)

1. *What the cross games bring to a mirror*: for each deck, `both` against
   `mirrors` by their shares against `all71` in that deck's mirror, at as
   many games of it, the deals paired: the cross games **bring** strength
   there when `both` is at least 2 points over by two standard errors,
   **cost** strength when it is at least 2 points under by two standard
   errors, otherwise **not told apart**. Against `all71`, an opponent
   common to both, and not head to head, where two runs of the recipe
   landed 3.6 and 7.3 points apart.
2. *The same games in one network*: `mirrors` against each deck's champion
   head to head in its mirror, at as many games of it, every deal from both
   seats: **matches** when its share less two standard errors is at least
   48%, **weaker** when its share plus two standard errors is at most 48%,
   otherwise **not decided**.

Told beside them, without a mark (`tools/two_decks_mirrors_report.py`):
`mirrors` against `both` head to head in each mirror; `mirrors` against
`all71` over each champion's own share there, the deals paired, which is
what the shared weights give at equal games; Blackwing against Cyber
against `all71`, beside `both`'s 63.5%; the hours.

### The build (2026-10-24)

- `--self-play-pairs {all,mirrors}` (`TrainerConfig.self_play_pairs`): with
  `mirrors` a learner of several decks plays its duels against itself with
  one deck in both seats, every deck as often as any other
  (`schedules.allocate_matchup_seats`). `all`, the default, is the
  allocation as it was, so every run before is unchanged; the duels
  against the pilots are the same either way, in the same places of an
  update (`tests/test_orchestration.py`).
- `experiments/commands/two-decks-mirrors.sh` and
  `tools/two_decks_mirrors_report.py`, held by
  `tests/test_two_decks_mirrors_report.py`; tried small on the development
  machine's processor, on top of a small try of `two-decks.sh` made again.

### Results (2026-10-24)

Run on the CUDA machine from 10:33 to 13:23 and measured by 13:47, at
`44cb0a43`'s code (the report and the run's metrics copied to
`analysis/two-decks/mirrors/`). Nothing failed: no duel stopped by the
engine or cut off, at most 0.64 GiB of the card in use.

1. **What the cross games bring to a mirror: not told apart in either.**
   Against `all71`, the deals paired: in the Blackwing mirror the network of
   both wins 67.8% and the network of the mirrors 67.6%, +0.3 ± 0.6 points;
   in the Cyber mirror 81.3% against 78.4%, +2.9 ± 0.5, with two standard
   errors taken away 1.9, just short of 2.
2. **The same games in one network: matches, on both decks.** Head to head,
   the network of the mirrors wins 52.4% ± 0.4% of the Blackwing mirror
   against Blackwing alone and 52.2% ± 0.4% of the Cyber mirror against
   `cyber-old`.

Beside them, without a mark:

- **Against `all71`, over each champion's own share**, the deals paired:
  +2.8 ± 0.6 points in the Blackwing mirror (67.6% against 64.8%), +2.0 ±
  0.5 in the Cyber mirror (78.4% against 76.4%).
- **Head to head with the network of both:** 48.4% of the Blackwing mirror,
  45.5% of the Cyber mirror.
- **Blackwing against Cyber, against `all71`:** 54.0% where the network of
  both wins 63.5%, 9.5 ± 0.6 points under it.
- **The hours:** 2.83 of PPO; its updates grew from 5.8 s to 8.2 s as its
  games against itself went from 13 to 16 turns.

What it says: given the two champions' games and nothing more, one network
of both decks matches each champion head to head and is ahead of each
against `all71`, so the decks' shared weights pay, at no cost in games. The
two decks played against each other add little to either mirror (not told
apart, nearly 3 points in the Cyber mirror) and much to their own matchup,
9.5 points of Blackwing against Cyber. The network of both owed its edge
over the champions in the mirrors mostly to the shared weights, and its
cross games to the matchup they are.

## Follow-up: a single-deck champion outside its mirror (readings fixed on 2026-10-24)

Taken up while the mirrors only trained: how well a single-deck champion
plays outside its mirror. If as well as a network of several decks,
single-deck champions could be made apart, on many machines at once; if
not, they would still need their pairings trained.

Part of it More decks answered on 2026-10-20. `decks-01` played Blackwing
against four of the thirteen decks held by networks of five and of thirteen
decks, 1.4 and 1.6 points better than the network of five, which had played
those pairings against itself: outside the mirror a network was no better
head to head than against the pilots. But `decks-01` had met those four
decks as pilots. What was not known: a champion against a deck it never
met, beside a network of several decks that is itself a champion. It was
measured before a test of four decks, as it bears on what that test would
be worth.

- **Blackwing against Cyber**, against `all71` as the opponent common to
  both, on the 2,048 deals the network of both was measured on. Blackwing
  alone, which never met Cyber, plays Blackwing against `all71` playing
  Cyber; `all71` plays Blackwing against `cyber-old`, which met Blackwing's
  pilot alone, playing Cyber. Each by its checkpoints after 480, 600 and 720
  updates of PPO, set against the network of both after 1,920, 2,400 and
  2,880, which had played the pairing against itself 374,400 times.
- **A panel beside it:** each champion playing its deck against `all71`
  playing each of the 58 decks outside the thirteen (57 for Cyber), 64
  games each (`eval_suites/blackwing-against-others-checkpoint.json`,
  `eval_suites/cyber-against-others-checkpoint.json`), and `all71` playing
  the champion's deck in the same games.
- **Played on the development machine's processor**
  (`experiments/commands/outside-the-mirror.sh`), the checkpoints copied
  from the CUDA machine. The network of both's games after 1,920 updates
  were played again here first: 2,048 of 2,048 end as they did there.

### Readings (fixed on 2026-10-24, before anything was played)

1. *Blackwing's side*: Blackwing alone against the network of both, each
   playing Blackwing against `all71` playing Cyber, checkpoint for
   checkpoint, the deals paired: the single-deck champion plays it **as
   well** when its share less the network of both's, less two standard
   errors, is above −2 points; **worse** when that difference plus two
   standard errors is below −2; otherwise **not decided**.
2. *Cyber's side*: the same with `cyber-old` against the network of both,
   each playing Cyber against `all71` playing Blackwing.

Told beside them, without a mark (`tools/outside_the_mirror_report.py`,
held by `tests/test_outside_the_mirror_report.py`): the panel, each
champion's edge over `all71` playing its deck, the games paired, by its
edge in its own mirror.

### Results (2026-10-24)

Played on the development machine's processor that morning
(`analysis/outside-the-mirror/`, its report in `report.txt`).

1. **Blackwing's side: worse.** Playing Blackwing against `all71` playing
   Cyber, Blackwing alone wins 72.6% of the games, the network of both
   83.9%: −11.3 ± 0.8 points, the deals paired.
2. **Cyber's side: worse.** Playing Cyber against `all71` playing Blackwing,
   `cyber-old` wins 31.7%, the network of both 43.1%: −11.4 ± 0.8.

Beside them, the panel: playing its deck against `all71` playing each deck
outside the thirteen, Blackwing alone wins 53.6% of the games where `all71`
playing Blackwing wins 60.6% (−7.0 ± 0.8 points over 58 decks), and
`cyber-old` 38.3% where `all71` playing Cyber wins 43.4% (−5.1 ± 0.8 over
57). In their own mirrors they are 14.8 and 26.4 points over `all71`.

What it says: a single-deck champion is a champion of its mirror alone.
Against a deck it never played, or met only as a pilot, it is well under a
network that played the pairing against itself, and under `all71` too,
which met every deck of the pool some hundreds of times. On 2026-10-20
`decks-01` was level with a network of five decks in pairings it had met as
pilots 38,400 times each, where that network had played them against
itself 39,900 times; here the network of both had played Blackwing against
Cyber 374,400 times. Champions made apart on many machines would still need
their pairings trained; the 4-deck test asks what that costs.
